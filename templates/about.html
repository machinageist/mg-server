Where this could lead — fields I'm drawn to

The SysAdmin/NOC work above comes first; everything in this section is subordinate to it and to getting the fundamentals right. But I'm in this for the long haul, and a few fields genuinely pull at me. These are interests and possible directions — not current skills, credentials, or commitments. They may be on the table as the foundation matures; none of them is a promise.

  • AI infrastructure & platform engineering — the natural horizon past operations: cloud, infrastructure-as-code, containers and Kubernetes, observability, and eventually the GPU / distributed-systems and high-performance-networking side of AI infrastructure. It builds directly on the Linux, networking, and homelab work, which is why it draws me most.
  • Open & heterogeneous hardware — FPGA, RISC-V, and photonic / optical interconnect, and how AI workloads actually run across different silicon. A curiosity I'd like to explore, not a claim.
  • Resilient & off-grid compute and comms — Meshtastic / LoRa mesh networks, cyberdecks, and solar-powered nodes: systems built to keep working off the grid.
  • Adaptive, secure, auditable AI systems — a longer-horizon research interest in agents that learn over time and can still be inspected and controlled. Honestly a moonshot — something worth pursuing eventually, not something I've started or can claim.
  • Robotics, edge AI, and biosensing — later, careful interests at the edges of the above, named as directions rather than plans.
  • Independent, self-directed work — over time, building a consultable infrastructure niche rather than only climbing an employed ladder. I don't have to choose the path yet.

If any of these becomes real, it will show up here as evidence held to the same standard as everything else — not as a title claimed ahead of the work.
